**En passant** ("in passing") is the strangest rule in chess — and plenty of beginners have never heard of it!

## When can you capture?

When an enemy pawn **moves two squares** from its starting rank and **lands right next to your pawn**, your pawn may capture it **as if it had moved only one square**.

<MoveSequence fen="4k3/3p4/8/4P3/8/8/8/4K3 b - - 0 1" moves="d5 exd6">Black plays d7–d5, landing next to the e5 pawn. White captures en passant: the e5 pawn goes to d6 and the d5 pawn is removed.</MoveSequence>

<Callout type="rule">
You may only capture **on the very next move**. If you play something else, the chance is gone for good.
</Callout>

<Exercise kind="make-move" fen="4k3/8/8/8/5pP1/8/8/4K3 b - g3 0 1" answers="f4g3" explain="fxg3 en passant: the f4 pawn captures diagonally onto g3 and the g4 pawn is removed.">
You are Black. White just played g2–g4. **Capture en passant**!
</Exercise>

<Callout type="fun">
This rule appeared when pawns were allowed to move two squares at first — so a pawn could not "sneak past" an enemy pawn without risk.
</Callout>
