Knowing a few **mating patterns** helps you finish games quickly and confidently.

## The back-rank mate

When a king sits behind its own pawns with **no escape square**, a rook or queen landing on the back rank is checkmate.

<Exercise kind="make-move" fen="6k1/5ppp/8/8/8/8/5PPP/4Q1K1 w - - 0 1" answers="e1e8" mate explain="Qe8#! The f7, g7 and h7 pawns block their own king's escape.">
White to move: **checkmate in 1**.
</Exercise>

<Callout type="tip">
To avoid back-rank mates, give your king some "luft" (breathing room), for example h3 (or h6 as Black).
</Callout>

## Queen and king together

A queen alone cannot mate a king. But with **your king's support**, a queen right next to the enemy king is mate.

<Board fen="k7/1Q6/1K6/8/8/8/8/8 b - - 0 1">The b7 queen is protected by the king on b6: checkmate!</Board>

<Exercise kind="make-move" fen="k7/8/1K6/8/8/8/8/2Q5 w - - 0 1" answers="c1c8" mate explain="Qc8#! But Qc7?? leaves the black king with no moves while not in check — stalemate!">
White to move: **checkmate in 1**. Watch out for stalemate!
</Exercise>

<Callout type="warning">
With only the enemy king left, first drive it to the **edge of the board** — mating in the middle is very hard.
</Callout>
